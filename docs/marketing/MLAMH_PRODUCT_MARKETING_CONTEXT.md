# MLAMH Product Marketing Context

Status: APPROVED BASELINE
Owner: MLAMH Marketing Growth Engine
Scope: Canonical marketing context for human and AI marketing work

## 1. Product definition
MLAMH is an AI-powered Talent & Opportunities Platform and talent infrastructure layer that connects qualified talent with publishers of real opportunities.

MLAMH is not positioned as a generic job board, CV database, or public contact directory.

Current core marketplace:
- Talent: actors and models in the current MVP, expanding later by market and category.
- Publishers: production companies, agencies, brands, event organizers, project owners, and other approved opportunity publishers.

Core liquidity loop:
Publisher -> Opportunity -> Applications -> Review -> Selection -> Connection

## 2. Core value propositions
### Talent
- Discover relevant, credible opportunities.
- Build a professional profile that can be discovered and matched.
- Apply through a structured workflow.
- Protect private contact and media access until governed connection conditions are met.

### Publisher
- Reach qualified talent supply instead of searching manually across fragmented channels.
- Publish structured opportunities and collect applications in one place.
- Review and shortlist candidates efficiently.
- Use MLAMH matching, qualification, and workflow tools to reduce casting and sourcing friction.

## 3. Positioning
Primary positioning:
AI-powered Talent & Opportunities Platform / Talent Infrastructure

Saudi-first positioning:
Started in Saudi Arabia. Built for the GCC and global expansion.

Do not describe MLAMH as only:
- a casting website,
- a recruitment site,
- an influencer directory,
- a social network.

These can be use cases, but not the full product definition.

## 4. Market strategy
- Saudi Arabia: primary active market and proof-of-liquidity market.
- UAE: first external expansion market when the Saudi core loop is sufficiently proven.
- Qatar: prepared expansion market.
- Additional markets are activated through the Multi-Country architecture, not through hard-coded one-off logic.

Marketing, segmentation, messaging, SEO, and analytics should be country-aware where relevant.

## 5. Audiences
### Supply audience
Actors, models, and later additional talent categories.

Primary needs:
- real opportunities,
- professional visibility,
- trust,
- privacy,
- simple application flow,
- fair access.

### Demand audience
Production companies, agencies, brands, event organizers, casting teams, creative teams, and project owners.

Primary needs:
- faster talent discovery,
- qualified supply,
- structured applications,
- lower sourcing friction,
- better response quality,
- governed communication.

## 6. Brand voice
MLAMH communication should be:
- direct,
- professional,
- modern,
- concise,
- credible,
- regionally aware,
- free of exaggerated promises.

Avoid:
- fake urgency,
- unverifiable claims,
- manipulative scarcity,
- describing every opportunity as a job when it is not,
- language that suggests MLAMH guarantees selection, income, or work.

Arabic should read naturally for Gulf audiences and not as a literal translation from English.
English should be concise and internationally understandable.

## 7. Trust and privacy rules
Marketing must never encourage public exposure of information that product policy protects.

Key principle:
A talent's protected contact details, private media, and governed links remain hidden from public users and are exposed only under approved product rules.

Marketing copy must not imply that publishers can freely scrape or contact all talent outside the governed workflow.

## 8. Growth model
MLAMH growth should optimize both sides of the marketplace.

Primary growth domains:
- Acquisition
- Activation
- Conversion
- Retention
- Referral
- Revenue readiness
- Marketplace liquidity

Supply-only growth is not enough. Demand growth is not enough. The objective is a healthy opportunity-to-application-to-selection loop.

## 10. Marketing operating principles
1. Reuse MLAMH systems before adding new tools.
2. Human approval and governed policies remain authoritative for sensitive actions.
3. Marketing Skills are knowledge and reasoning inputs, not autonomous authority.
4. No agent should bypass MLAMH APIs or governance to perform sensitive production writes.
5. Prefer measurable loops over one-off campaigns.
6. Separate facts from hypotheses and experiments.
7. Record learnings so future campaigns improve instead of restarting from zero.
8. Avoid duplicate automation paths for email, social, outreach, or analytics.
9. Country, language, channel, and audience context should be explicit where they affect outcomes.
10. Cost-efficient execution is preferred: batch related changes and avoid unnecessary deployment cycles.

## 11. Zero-cost and low-cost growth priority
Until paid acquisition is strategically justified, prioritize:
- SEO and programmatic discovery,
- talent-led sharing and referral loops,
- opportunity-led distribution,
- founder-led outreach,
- direct B2B prospecting,
- partnerships and co-marketing,
- organic social content,
- useful free tools and lead magnets,
- lifecycle email and follow-up.

Paid media is an optional scale lever, not the foundation of the growth model.

## 12. AI and automation rules
AI can:
- research,
- classify,
- draft,
- score,
- summarize,
- recommend,
- generate experiments,
- surface anomalies,
- prepare governed actions.

AI must not automatically perform high-risk or externally consequential actions when MLAMH policy requires approval.

Examples requiring governed handling include:
- sensitive outbound communication,
- commercial commitments,
- partnership terms,
- material pricing changes,
- destructive data actions,
- policy exceptions.

## 14. Success criteria
The Marketing Growth Engine succeeds when it can answer, with data:
- Which channels acquire qualified talent?
- Which channels acquire qualified publishers?
- Which messages produce replies and briefs?
- Which opportunities produce qualified applications?
- Where users drop from signup to activation?
- Which markets and categories have supply gaps?
- Which referrals create active users?
- Which experiments materially improve the marketplace loop?

The system should continuously move from activity metrics toward business and marketplace outcomes.
